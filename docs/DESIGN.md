# Design

> **Direction change (2026-10-08, D-019).** This file describes the game as built today; sections are rewritten as each milestone lands. Since 0.5.0 (M2a) the world is 512 tall, dwarven holds are rare and vast, the deep is natural caves over a lava sea, and old lights are cold. Since 0.6.0 (M2b) there are ways down, varied places underground, other peoples' remains, ruined surface sites, old roads and waystones. Still to come: the dwindling age's lived-in places and peoples, and more. See `docs/DIRECTION_QA.md` and `docs/MILESTONES.md`.

## Setting and pillars

- A Tolkien-esque world in a **dwindling age** (D-019): ancient ruins everywhere, a few dwarven holds still kept up, and lived-in places and travelers to come in later milestones. No machines or modern equipment.
- **Original names only.** Places evoke the feel of that kind of setting without using Tolkien's names, which are still under copyright.
- Everything built by the old peoples is **ancient, decaying and decrepit**: broken walls, rubble, fallen pillars, dead lamps, dust and bones.
- The underground is deep and layered; going down should feel like going back in time.
- **The realms of men** (D-060): kingdoms of high men hold about half the stretches of the gentle old lands, living and kept up: walled cities in white stone, castles, villages, farms, watchtowers and roads, with their soldiers and townsfolk, neutral unless attacked.
- **The high elves** (D-062): the elder realms hold about half the stretches of the mountains, high meadows, crags and sea cliffs: hidden cities terraced into the mountainsides, fastnesses, refuges, watch-spires and gardens in elvenstone under verdigris and shingle, with a strong host of warriors and knights and their folk, neutral unless attacked.
- **The wood elves** (D-065): the forest kingdoms hold about half the stretches of the Elder Wood, Autumn Woods, Birch Glades, Ancient Giant Wood, Yew Wood and Silverwood: cities up in great silver-barked, golden-crowned trees, the king's halls under a hill, tree-villages round feasting glades, watch-trees and glades, on the ground as it lies with the forest growing on among them, with wardens, guards and their folk, neutral unless attacked.
- **The mountain dwarves** (D-066): the High Mountains the high elves leave, and half the stretches of the Pine Highlands and the Glacier Fields, are mountain kingdoms: holds on terraces under a massif with their great gate, and under them halls, forges, a cistern and mines (more worked below than built above); bastions, mining towns, lookouts and mines; engines of war and tools of the age; their folk short, broad and strong in arms, neutral unless attacked.
- **The riders of the plains** (D-061): kingdoms of horse-lords hold about half the stretches of the open lands (steppe, moors, barrow hills): hill towns in terraces behind palisades, holds, steadings, horse farms, beacons and roads, with guards, horsemen and their folk, neutral unless attacked.
- **Foes only in the fire** (D-052, the owner's revisit of Q46): orcs, trolls, goblins and the Emberlords are hostile, and all of them belong to the Volcanic Wastes and what lies under them. Everywhere else creatures stay peaceful. Death stays forgiving (your things wait in a grave), and in creative the foes take no notice of you.
- **Lava and the orcs' places** (D-064): lava runs down most of the volcanoes' gullies; war camps, watchtowers, forges and fighting pits of the orcs stand across the wastes' floor.
- **The peoples against the wastes** (D-063): the folk of the realms and the creatures of the wastes are foes to each other wherever they meet; hosts of the nearest people march through the wastes against them.

These twelve are the looks the world draws today. Since 0.15.0 (M6a, D-039) they are worn by the lands of the land registry (`world/lands.js`), which holds the 7 old lands kept, 3 old lands reworked (Windswept Plains into Golden Steppe, Fens into Willow Vales, Northern Fells into Frozen Tundra) and 27 more new ones: 37 lands in all, in seven families. The Grey Shore and the Lake are edges, not lands. **The full list, the transition map and the layout picture are in `docs/LANDS.md`, generated by `npm run lands`.**

**The forests (M6b, D-040):** Autumn Woods (red and golden maples, leaf litter), Birch Glades (birch groves and bluebell glades), Pine Highlands (tall pines on hilly ground, needle floors, snow on the cold heights), Ancient Giant Wood (rare; greatwoods with buttress roots, moss and ferns), Willow Vales (the Fens reworked; willows over pools and reeds), Yew Wood (broad dark yews, moss) and Silverwood (white trees with silver leaves, faintly glowing moonpetals). Each stretch has its landmark, its natural feature, or (four in ten) neither: woodcutters' lodge or red-leaf glade; birch-bark shrine or ring of white birches; timber watch post or lookout rock; platform in a giant or the Great Tree; stilt house or willow pools; archers' hall or hollow yew; moon gate or silver spring.

**Highlands and cold (M6c, D-041):** Alpine Meadows (high pastures with gentians and edelweiss), Glacier Fields (glacier ice and packed snow), Cloud Forest Heights (gnarled mistwood over moss and ferns), Karst Crags (limestone ridges and pillars) and Frozen Tundra (the Northern Fells reworked; snow, tundra moss, stunted spruces). Signatures: shepherd's hut or meadow tarn; ice-bound tower or ice cave; mist shrine or mossy falls; cliff hermitage or a great sinkhole down to the caves; frozen longhouse or frost mounds; and in the High Mountains a gatehouse in a high pass or a high tarn. Waterfalls cascade down steep high ground to pools.

**Coasts and waters (M6d, D-042):** Chalk Cliffs (white cliffs dropping sheer to the sea), Rocky Isles (islands of rock and turf), Fjords (sea inlets cut deep into mountains), Black Sand Shores (black beaches, basalt), Kelp Shallows (shallow floors under kelp and seagrass) and Raised Bogs (peat, bog moss, cotton grass). Kelp and seagrass grow inside the water, lily pads float on lakes and pools, and driftwood lies on the Grey Shore. Signatures: wreck or sea stacks (the Western Sea); isle chapel or sea arch; sunken causeway or kelp forest; beacon tower or chalk arch; boathouse or fjord fall; harbour wall or basalt columns; trackway or domed bog.

**Dry and fiery (M6e, D-043):** Southern Drylands (dunes, red sand, sandstone, cactus), Golden Steppe (the Windswept Plains reworked; golden grass), Volcanic Wastes (remade in 0.27.0, below) and Blighted Lands (dead grass, grey dead trees). Signatures: sand-buried temple or dry wadi with a well; ring of horse stones or lone outcrop; the Ashen Citadel or the Rift of Fire; dead lord's hall or grey grove.

**Strange lands (M6f, D-044, rare):** Crystal Barrens (amethyst spires, prism shards), Glowcap Hollows (giant glowing mushrooms), Petrified Forest (stone trees with amber) and Starfall Craters (a crater field of scorched stone, starmetal in its hearts). Signatures: crystal cutters' ruin or crystal spires; mushroom dwelling or giant glowcaps; waystation of stone or grove of stone trees; star tower or the crater of the starmetal heart.

**Old lands of men (M6g, D-045):** Overgrown Farmland (old furrows of wild wheat, turnips and potatoes), Wild Orchards (fruit trees in rows, blossom, apples), Flower Meadows (bands of wildflowers) and Old Terraces (hillsides stepped every three blocks, stone retaining walls). Signatures: manor farm or old furrows; orchard keeper's cottage or old fruit rows; flower shrine or bands of wildflowers; terraced village or stone terraces. Across many lands stand small structures of the men of old: bridges, mills, jetties, farmsteads, cairns, camps, beacon hills, chapels with graveyards; dry-stone walls cross the farmland and hills. Landmarks, features and small structures are named in the readout and kept as places on the map.

**Ruins, water and weather (M6h, D-046):** watchtowers, keeps and castles come in four layouts each, face four ways and are more or less ruined. Ponds and springs lie in the green and wooded lands, rocks make rapids in the rivers, and hillside streams run down the hills. Weather follows the land: mist on the bogs, vales and Cloud Forest, storms on coasts and mountains, snow in the cold lands, dust in the drylands, ash in the volcanic and blighted lands, and skies tinted to match. Caves near the surface take on the land above them. The old kept lands have their landmarks and features: an earthwork hill fort and the lone oak (Green Hills), a woodland shrine and a hollow elder (Elder Wood), a watch cairn and granite tors (Heath Moors), a great chambered barrow and rows of standing stones (Barrow Hills), a temple sunk in the roots and the fallen giant (Shadowed Forest).

**Lore and chronicles (M7, D-047):** every dwarven hold has a chronicle of eight pages, one on each lectern, from its founding to its fall or to the present day. The journal (L) keeps the pages in order, marks the missing ones, and holds the sayings of the rune tablets you have read. The discovery log records lands, layers, holds, remains and relics. None of it is a goal: the game still works with no goal at all. The texts are drafts in `docs/LORE.md`.

**Sound (M8, D-048):** all synthesized, no music. Master, effects and ambience volumes in the pause menu. Each land has a soundscape by day and by night (birds, crickets, surf and gulls, frogs, wind, rumble and lava, chimes, creaks), and each layer underground its own (drips, rumble, lava near the fire, hammers in an inhabited hold). Sounds come from where they happen, and running water from its direction.

**Wildlife (E1, D-049):** deer, rabbits, sheep, mountain goats, wild hens, boars, wild horses, stray hounds and wild mules live in the lands that suit them. They wander and graze, and the shy ones flee a running player; none attacks. Hunting gives meat and hides; shears give wool, an empty hand milk and eggs. A wild horse can be ridden. A hound fed meat and a mule fed crops follow you; the mule carries a pack like a chest. Since 0.26.0 (D-050) each kind has a pixel-box model with painted coats (several per kind), young in the herds, a gait, grazing, sleep at night, herd fright, a call of its own and a shadow.

**The realms of men (D-060):** the first of the living kingdoms, high men of a southern realm. Their stone is white and their livery black; their sign is an eight-pointed star over a white tower, carved over their gates, painted on their shields and breasts and flown on their banners. About half the stretches of Green Hills, Overgrown Farmland, Wild Orchards, Flower Meadows and Old Terraces are held. A held stretch is a province, named in the realm's style, and every few provinces form a kingdom, one of them holding the royal city.
- **The city** stands near the province's middle, walled in white stone eleven high with towers and banners. Its gatehouse has the portcullis drawn up and the sign over the arch. Its streets are paved and climb in tiers by stairs under arches to the citadel. There is a market square with a fountain and stalls, and houses of white stone or timbered plaster, two and three storeys under slate, glazed and lit, with floors, ladders and stores inside. Among them stand gardens, barracks and a training yard, a smithy, stables and the domed Hall of Lamps. In the citadel are a statue of a king of old, the great hall with its pillared porch, and the white tower crowned with a slate spire and a banner.
- **The castle** keeps the border: a curtain wall with towers and a gatehouse, a keep of four storeys with turrets, barracks, stables, a yard and a well.
- **Villages** gather round a green with a thatched well. Their cottages are of plaster and timber on cobble under thatch, with an inn, a chapel of white stone with its bell tower and a smithy, and haystacks and hedged fields about them.
- **Farmsteads** have a farmhouse, a barn and fields of wheat, potatoes, turnips and beans. **Watchtowers** stand on the heights, a beacon on each roof.
- **Roads:** the king's roads are paved and kerbed in white stone, with a lamp-topped milestone every 72 blocks, and lanes run to the villages and farms. Both bridge the rivers.
- **Relief:** no wall is a flat face. Stone stands on rock-faced plinths with quoins, its windows set back over sills, moulded courses at each floor and cornices on corbels; timbered storeys jut out over the lanes; roofs have dormers and verges with finials; towers and walls carry their parapets out on corbels over arrow slits and loops, buttressed; a city's tiers stand on rough-stone retaining walls under copings.
- **Soldiers** (painted at twice the detail of other creatures, their mail in rings and plate riveted) wear black surcoats over mail with the white sign, silver hems and tall crested helms: spearmen and swordsmen with black kite shields, archers with longbows, captains with black cloaks and white plumes. **Townsfolk** dress by their trade: townsmen and townswomen, merchants, smiths, farmers and lords of the realm.
- **Their ways:** they keep their places, guards at the gates, merchants at the market and farmers in their fields, and patrols walk the king's roads. They stop for a passer-by and greet them by the name of their town. They are neutral: strike one, and the guards near by take up arms while the townsfolk flee, until their wrath passes. Never in creative.
- **Around them:** towns sound of voices, carts, a bell and a forge. The readout names each place and the kingdom that holds it.

**The riders of the plains (D-061):** the second people of the realms of men, horse-lords of the open lands. They build in timber, wattle and fieldstone under golden thatch and turf, carve and gild their halls, and set crossed horse heads on their gables; their livery is green and gold over mail, their sign a white horse running on green. About half the stretches of the Golden Steppe, the Heath Moors and the Barrow Hills are held, as provinces of kingdoms named "The Riders of" a founder, one of them holding the king's seat.
- **The hill town** stands on a hill in round terraces (three at the king's seat) behind a palisade of sharpened logs on a fieldstone bank over a ditch, with a walk behind it and timber towers under golden thatch along it; two more flank the gate under a carved beam with the horse heads. A way of fieldstone climbs by stairs between carved posts and lamps to the golden hall on the top terrace: carved and gilded oak on a terrace of its own, a pillared porch, a gilded ridge, and within, carved pillars, a long hearth, tapestries and shields, and the high seat under banners. Longhouses of wattle and daub under turf and thatch stand in a ring round each terrace facing its lane, each with its hearth. By the gate are stables, a paddock of horses, a smithy, stalls and a well. Six kings' mounds line the road before the king's seat.
- **Holds** keep the border: a round wall of fieldstone over a ditch, round gate towers capped in thatch, a round keep with a gallery round its top, a hall, stables and a ring for training horses.
- **Steadings** gather longhouses and a mead hall round a green with a thatched well, with a smithy, a paddock, haystacks and a fence of wattle hurdles, fields about them. **Horse farms** have a long stable under turf, a farmhouse and paddocks of horses. **Beacons** burn on the heights inside a ring of stakes, with a lookout and a turf hut.
- **Roads:** the riders' roads are beaten earth with gravel in the ruts and grass on the crown, a carved post with the horse heads every 72 blocks, timber bridges over the rivers; cart tracks run to the steadings and farms.
- **Guards** go in long mail to the knee under green cloaks bordered in gold, in tall helms with a gilt band and a tail of white horsehair, with ash spears under green pennons and round green shields bearing the white horse. **Bowmen** wear quilted coats and carry short horn bows; **marshals** gilded helms, swords and horns. **Horsemen** ride saddled horses in green saddlecloths, galloping when they chase and riding the roads in patrols. **Freemen, freewomen, horse-herds and thanes** wear woven borders, embroidered gowns and braids, hoods and crooks, green and gold.
- **Their ways** are those of the white realm's folk: neutral until one is struck, with their own greetings and a low horn when they take up arms. Horses in their paddocks stay there until ridden away. Their towns sound of voices, horses, a forge and a horn.

**The high elves (D-062):** the elder realms of the high elves, in pale elvenstone and gilded armour, their sign a silver star of six rays over two leaves. About half the stretches of the High Mountains, Alpine Meadows, Karst Crags, Fjords and Chalk Cliffs are held, as provinces of realms named "The Elder Realm of" a founder, one holding the high seat. They need no level ground: a site keeps the lie of its land, cut into terraces that follow it, each building on a platform of its own; where a terrace or platform stands over the ground beside it, a retaining wall of elvenstone courses under a carved coping carries a gilt rail.
- **The hidden city** climbs its mountainside inside a wall with tall merlons and a tower every thirty blocks. Its gate is a pointed arch between two towers, a banner over it and two statues of warriors before it, their hands on swords set point down. The way climbs by stairs between parapets, lamps on columns beside it, to the high hall: two storeys of tall windows, an arcaded porch, a great tower beside it. Halls stand about the terraces facing the view, their bays of stained glass under pointed tracery, their balconies of filigree, their roofs of verdigris copper or dark shingle swept up from low eaves, crested in gilt and turned up at the corners. Slender towers rise where the land is steepest, with galleries and open belvederes under swept cones. There are pavilions under domes at the viewpoints, pools with lamps standing in the water, gardens, and silver-leaved and blossoming trees on every lawn. By the gate stand barracks, an armoury and a training ground.
- **Fastnesses** hold the borders: a great tower in a high-walled court with towers, a garrison hall and a training ground. **Refuges** gather halls round a house with a porch, a pavilion, a pool and a garden. **Watch-spires** stand on the heights. **Gardens** are orchards of silver leaf and blossom with a summer pavilion.
- **Roads:** the elven ways are paved between kerbs, with lamps on columns; hidden paths of gravel and stepping stones; bridges on slender piers with gilt rails.
- **Warriors** (taller than men, pointed of ear, long-haired) go in gilded armour of leaf-shaped scales over long wine-red robes, under close helms with a blade of a crest: spearwardens with glaives, bladewardens with curved blades and tall leaf-shaped shields, archers with tall bows and long cloaks, lord-captains in blue cloaks with the star. **Knights** ride horses in long blue caparisons with gilded chanfrons, lances pennoned. **Their folk:** elf-lords in robes with hanging sleeves and gold vines, elf-ladies in flowing gowns with circlets and gems, loremasters with their books, smiths.
- **Their ways:** stronger in arms than men and many at their gates, they are still neutral until one is struck; they greet a passer-by in their own words and sound a clear high horn when they take up arms. Their cities sound of a harp, distant singing and chimes.

**The peoples against the wastes (D-063):** men, riders and elves are foes of the orcs, trolls, goblins and Emberlords, and they of them, wherever they meet (in creative too, so a player can set them against each other with "Bring a foe").
- Each looks about for the nearest of the other side it can see, closes in and strikes or shoots; a fight rallies its fellows near by, and townsfolk flee. A blow reaches a big creature's near side, so a bladewarden can wound a troll or an Emberlord.
- The folk never turn on a player who does them no harm: their blows and shots strike only the other side. The creatures' shots still strike a player in survival. A creature the player strikes turns on the player; what falls to the other side leaves the player nothing (an Emberlord felled by the folk stays fallen).
- **Hosts:** every few minutes a host of the people whose held lands lie nearest (four to six warriors with their captain; the riders' mostly horsemen, the elves' with a knight) marches through the Volcanic Wastes from out of sight behind the player to as far again beyond, and falls upon the warbands it meets. The hosts keep to the wastes' open ground, so hostile creatures still never leave the wastes. A host greets a passer-by in its own words and turns on a player who strikes one of it.

**Lava down the gullies and the orcs' places (D-064):** the gullies scored down every volcano's flanks have flat floors, and most carry a stream of lava from high on the flank down to the foot, a block down between steep sides; the rest have floors of glowing crust. None runs down the quarter the mountain's fortress faces. Out on the open floor of the wastes stand the orcs' places, each with its name and its orcs:
- **War camps:** a ring of black palisade (charred logs bound in iron, sharpened stakes along the top, iron spikes at its foot, banners) with a gateway between two towers with platforms, spikes, fire baskets and banners; tents of hide round a fire pit with poles of skulls about it; the warchief's tent at the back with banners and fire baskets; racks of spears, a cage, crates, barrels and bones. Orcs at the fire and at the gate, sometimes under a warchief, some asleep by night.
- **Watchtowers:** a foot of cracked basalt with a door, a stage of palisade and riveted iron, a platform with spikes and fire baskets under a pitched roof of hide, a chain to climb, poles of skulls and a ring of stakes. Bowmen on the platform, raiders at the foot.
- **Forges:** a yard of basalt tiles with a smelter banded in ember bricks, glowing at its mouth under a chimney; a trough of lava held in basalt; anvils of riveted iron under an awning of hide hung with chains; racks of blades, heaps of slag and scrap, stacks of charred wood, a fence of stakes. Brutes and raiders work it.
- **Fighting pits:** a pit four deep walled in basalt, iron spikes along its rim, cages in its wall, a chain to climb out, poles of skulls and fire baskets about it. Raiders and brutes fight in it, now and then a troll.
New blocks (creative, Building): Black Palisade and Riveted Iron.

**The wood elves (D-065):** the forest kingdoms, a people of the woods who are half merry woodland folk (feasting in glades, a king in halls under a hill with a cellar of casks, a crown of autumn leaves) and half the keepers of a golden wood (great silver-barked trees with golden crowns, platforms and stairs high in them, lamps of pale light everywhere, a city hidden behind its hedge). Their sign is a golden leaf on green. About half the stretches of the Elder Wood, Autumn Woods, Birch Glades, Ancient Giant Wood, Yew Wood and Silverwood are held, as provinces of kingdoms named "The Forest Kingdom of" a founder; the Pinewoods, the Willow Vales and the Shadowed Forest are left wild. They leave the ground as it lies, save a few pads and the hill over the king's halls, and the forest's own trees grow on between their works (kept off their paths, hedges, the goldboughs and the bridges overhead).
- **The goldbough** is their tree: a trunk of silver bark three to five across, flaring into roots, a crown of golden leaves high above. Round its trunk at shared heights across the city stand platforms of pale planks edged in carved wood with woven rails and lamps at the quarters; on the higher ones a house wraps round the trunk under a cone of leaf thatch, with windows, a door toward the stair and lamps within. A stair winds up the trunk from its foot to its highest platform, with a carved edge, a rail and lamps. Bridges of planks with woven rails run from platform to platform between neighbouring trees.
- **The city in the trees** stands behind a clipped hedge, its gate a carved beam on posts hung with the banner of the wood between two goldboughs, a bridge over the way between them. A lamplit way of leaf litter runs from the gate to the king's tree at the back, the greatest of all, with an open platform, a house and the king's hall high in it; paths lead off to the foot of every stair. Goldboughs stand about the city on a loose grid; there is a spring in a ring of rootstone with a lamp in its middle and an archery range by the way.
- **The king's halls** lie under a hill raised over them. Before the hill's face is a forecourt crossed by a stream under a bridge, with lamps along the way. The gate is a pointed arch of rootstone between carved pillars, its carved doors standing open and the banner over it. Within is a great hall on pillars like trees branching into the vault, lamps on them, the king's seat of carved wood on a dais at its back crowned with branching antlers; through doors in its sides lie a cellar of casks and a guardroom. Goldboughs stand on the hill's shoulders.
- **Tree-villages** gather four or five goldboughs in a ring round a feasting glade, houses on their higher platforms, bridges between neighbours, a house or two of carved wood on the ground. **Watch-trees** stand alone with a low platform and a high one. **Glades** are clearings ringed with lamps, long tables with benches and casks round a fire, a house at the edge.
- **Roads:** lamplit ways of leaf litter edged in moss with a lamp on a carved post every 32 blocks; woodland paths; bridges of planks with woven rails on posts of bark.
- **Their folk** (taller than men, pointed of ear, long-haired): **wardens** in hooded grey-green cloaks over soft leather with leaf-shaped copper plates at the shoulder and forearm, with long bows of pale wood (they see and shoot farther than any other folk); **blades** with two long knives curving like leaves (the quickest strikers); **guards of the halls** in armour of overlapping copper leaves over long green robes, leaf-crested helms and glaives; **captains** with the golden leaf on a green cloak. **The king** goes in long robes of silver and moss under a crown of autumn leaves and red berries, a staff of pale wood in hand; **lords** in green with silver leaves at the brow, **ladies** in gowns of white, silver or pale green with long braided hair, and **the folk of the glades** in green and brown with their hoods thrown back. Wardens keep the gates, the platforms and the watch-trees; guards keep the halls; the folk feast in the glades. Neutral until one is struck, they greet a passer-by in their own words and sound three rising notes on a horn when they take up arms. Never hostile in creative.
- **Sound:** their places sound of birds, distant singing, a harp and voices by day, and singing, crickets and owls by night.
New blocks (creative, under Building and Wood and plants): Goldbough Bark, Goldbough Planks, Goldbough Leaves, Woodland Lamp, Woven Rail, Carved Wood, Rootstone, Clipped Hedge, Banner of the Wood, Leaf Thatch.

**The mountain dwarves (D-066):** the mountain kingdoms, a people of miners, smiths and soldiers who live on and in the mountains, in dressed granite, bronze and firebrick, their sign a golden anvil under three white peaks on deep blue. They hold the High Mountains the high elves leave (the two peoples share that land between them) and one stretch in two of the Pine Highlands and the Glacier Fields. Their sites are cut into terraces like the elves', each building on a platform. The old dwarven ruins are another matter, left for later.
- **The mountain hold:** a wall of dressed granite four thick steps with the land round the front of the hold, square towers along it with ballistae on their platforms, a gatehouse of two great towers with a portcullis. The way climbs the terraces by stairs between lamps on columns to a forecourt under a massif of rock at the back, its face sheer and dressed in courses: the great gate stands open between columns under a knotwork lintel with the sign over it, and two colossal kings of stone hold their axes before it. On the terraces: houses of granite under steep slate with firebrick chimneys, smithies, smelting furnaces of firebrick glowing at their mouths and tops beside heaps of slag, a headframe of timber with its sheave wheel over the city's mine, a treadwheel crane, trebuchets behind the gate, barracks.
- **Under the mountain:** behind the gate the gate hall with its bronze doors open, then the great hall on two rows of great columns, long tables down its nave, lamps hung from its vault, the king's seat of granite, bronze and gold on a dais with braziers and banners; barracks off it; the great shaft with its winding drum, its hoist's chain and a ladder going down through every level. A stair beside the great hall goes down to the forge hall: a channel of molten iron down its middle, forges of firebrick along its walls with bellows, quench barrels and anvils, carts of ore, chains from the vault; beside it the cistern, its water held in granite with columns standing in it. Deep down the smelting hall, its great furnaces glowing and their channels running across the floor, and the mines: timbered galleries with lamps and ore carts, ore showing in their walls, side drifts off them. Every working is walled all round, so no cave's water or lava reaches in. More is carved under a hold than stands over it.
- **Bastions** guard the borders: a squat keep at the back of a walled court with towers at its corners, ballistae on them all, a trebuchet, a smithy; a stair cut down into the court leads to the garrison's hall under the keep and the armoury. **Mining towns** gather granite houses on the terraces round a headframe over a shaft down to two levels of mines, with a smelting furnace and a smithy. **Lookouts** are square towers on the heights with a ballista, a beacon and the banner. **Mines** are headframes over their shafts with a level of galleries below and heaps of spoil.
- **Roads:** stone roads paved in setts between kerbs of courses with a lamp on a column every 40 blocks; miners' tracks; bridges of granite on piers.
- **Tools and arms of the age:** the wheels, cranes, hoists, bellows and engines are built of blocks and do nothing; nothing is powered or runs on rails.
- **Their folk** (a block and a half tall, broad, big-nosed, bearded to the belt, the ends braided and bound in bronze): **axeguards** in mail under the blue tabard with round helms, bearded axes and round shields; **hammerers** in plate with masked helms and great hammers, the heaviest blows of any folk under a captain; **crossbowmen** in kettle hats with crossbows that hit hard and reload slowly; **captains** in winged helms and blue cloaks with gilded axes. **The Mountain King** goes in crimson and gold under a crown, his beard white, a rune staff in hand; **thanes** in fur-trimmed robes with circlets and chains of office, **ladies** in long banded dresses with their braids in loops, **miners** with lamps on their caps and picks, **smiths** in aprons with their arms bare. Guards keep the gates and crossbowmen the towers; hammerers stand before the great gate and in the great hall with the king; smiths work the forges above and below; miners work the headframes and the mines. Neutral until struck, never hostile in creative; their horn sounds two deep notes.
- **Sound:** hammers on anvils, voices, carts, picks at the rock, a deep chant now and then over the mountain's rumble.
New blocks (creative, Building): Dwarven Granite, Granite Courses, Knotwork Granite, Hold Column, Bronze Plating, Forge Brick, Molten Iron, Hold Lamp, Iron Cogwheel, Ore Cart, Slag, Pit Prop, Banner of the Mountain, Anvil, Hold Flagstones.

**Foes, warrens and fortresses (D-052):** the wastes are hostile ground. Orc warbands roam them (raiders with cleavers, bowmen who keep their distance, shield-bearing brutes, now and then a horned warchief whose horn brings the band) and hold the Ashen Citadel; ash trolls, three blocks tall with lava in the cracks of their hide, swing basalt clubs and throw rocks. Under the wastes the crawlways and upper caves give way to **goblin warrens**: six to nine halls on two levels cut in black rock seamed with magma, joined by tunnels propped with charred timber, each hall a den of hide huts round a fire pit, a store of crates and barrels, a pen of iron grates, a forge, a fire shrine with an obsidian idol, a hall crossed by a channel of lava, and the chief's hall with an obsidian throne on a dais and its hoard. Goblin cutters, slingers and firecallers live there under their chieftain, some asleep in the dens; a goblin camp of tents, skull poles, banners and stakes stands before each way in. A way down joins the caves below. The wastes lie **sunken** (D-055): each basin is at least 800 blocks across, its floor about 90 below the lands around, walled by a ring of ash mountains about 90 above them, so the way in climbs some 90 blocks to a jagged crest and falls some 180 down cliffs and shelves to the floor; the rivers of the floor run with lava at its own level, and nothing inside the ring is water. Each basin of the wastes has one **great volcano** near its middle (D-054, D-055; one to a basin since D-067), about 250 blocks high and 500 across, rising some 70 above the ring, with lesser cones about its foot. No volcano is a perfect cone (D-056): each is lopsided with its crater off its middle, steeper on some sides, scored by uneven gullies and toothed at the rim, and a great one has a scar where a flank fell away, a second summit and a shoulder cone; from afar a hazed stand-in of the basin and its volcano shows beyond the loaded world. In it stands **the Ember Throne**, an Emberlord's fortress built to its lord's size and in its own masonry (D-058): cracked and rough basalt bricks with seams that still glow, polished tiles, carved panels, burning script, obsidian bricks, fluted pillars, chains, fire baskets and spikes. A stair between skull-topped walls climbs to a deep terrace crossed by a moat of fire under a bridge lined with fire baskets, with obelisks at its corners. Two guardians forty-two blocks tall stand in the lord's likeness: hands on a great sword set point down, wings folded and edged with fire, horned, eyes burning. The gate is a pointed arch nineteen wide in rings of obsidian, fire and carving, with fangs at its mouth and a portcullis drawn up into it. Above it is the face of a demon (a heavy brow over slanted burning eyes, horns rising over the battlements), between towers fifty-eight high with battered feet, slits lit from within, machicolations, spiked battlements, horned corners and pools of fire on their roofs. The way in through the rock is a coffered pointed vault on ribs of fire, past troughs of lava, fire baskets, niches of skulls and fire hung on chains. Under the summit lies a vast throne hall, eighty wide and fifty high. Pointed vaults ribbed with fire and sunk in coffers (fire burns in every other one) span its nave and aisles over compound pillars joined by arcades, with cornices and fire baskets at their springing. Carved blind arches with fires at their feet line the side walls, sunk lancets the end walls, and cages of fire hang on chains. No great wall or ceiling of the fortress is flat: its towers are grooved, the way in is coffered and coursed, and its facade carries ledges and panels. Kerbed rivers of lava run in the floor, bridged in every bay, and lesser guardians stand in the aisles. The dais's steps are written in fire, and on it stand the throne, peaked and horned, and the hoard. Behind them, on the back wall, burn the lord's sign and columns of lava behind grates. There the Emberlord waits, a demon of shadow and flame twelve blocks tall, built of angled, tapering shapes (D-056): hunched over great clawed legs, shoulders broad over a narrow waist, spines down its back, a horned skull thrust forward and narrowing to the muzzle, slanted burning eyes under a heavy V of brow, fangs and a burning maw, a mane of flame streaming back, and vast wings of tapering bones and torn membrane spread behind it, their edges smouldering; it carries a burning blade and a whip of fire, and the ground shakes as it walks with a heavy rolling stride. It never goes farther than the terrace before its gate. Orcs keep its gate. A fallen Emberlord stays fallen. The lesser volcanoes hold **warholds** of the orcs, the same plan and detail at a smaller size, with a warchief on the throne. The warrens and fortresses glow red inside, with drums and chatter in the rock. Foes see, chase, strike, shoot and throw; goblins flee when badly hurt; a brute's shield takes most of a blow from the front. They leave iron, coal, leather, sulphur, gold and, from the Emberlord, the Flamebrand and the Emberheart.

**The war at the gates of the wastes (D-067):** each basin of the wastes has one great volcano and one way in, the Ashen Gate, on the side facing the nearest realm. The ring of ash mountains is cut through down to the level of the lands outside; across the cut the orcs have built a wall of basalt between two towers 64 high, one leaf shut and propped, the other hanging ajar with a way through by its edge. Inside, the yard behind the gate holds the orcs' camp (barricades, a palisade, the warlord's hall, tents, a forge, a troll's cage, a war drum, stone-throwers), and from its inner corner a broad road, the Long Descent, runs down along the ring's inner wall at one in eight to the floor. Outside, the dwarves' ram stands at the shut leaf under its bronze shed, a covered way of mantlets runs back to the siege lines (ditch, rampart, palisade, towers, ballistae, trebuchets, a siege tower), and beyond them lies the Leaguer, the camp of the allied hosts: 250 blocks square, more than three times the largest city of men, walled, gated and laid out in streets, with the Pavilion of the Three Banners in the middle, the men's tents, halls and stables, the elves' pavilions, spire and range, the dwarves' granite redoubt with its forges and engines, and the stores and field hospital. Both camps are manned, and while the player is near the two sides keep sending raids and scouts at each other through the gap, and every five to seven minutes a massive army (some 115 at the Massive size of the Battles setting): the hosts muster in ranks on the camp's field or in its square, men, dwarves and elves each in their block, and at the horns march out rank by rank to storm the Gatewarden's Camp and press on down the Long Descent; the wastes' warhost comes up the road company by company, masses before the gate and charges the lines and the camp. Each side calls its defenders to its lines when the other comes. They march by waypoints, fight where they meet and march home, and never turn on a player in creative. Far off, every creature is drawn as one mesh. Since W1 (D-068) every gate the player finds has its war, saved and moving on with play anywhere: the ram on the barred leaf, the dwarves' mine under the ring, the assaults on the walls and the orcs' sallies broken grow together over about an hour and a half of play and with the battles won and lost, shown on a war panel near the gate, on the world map and in the journal's chronicle. When armies meet the hosts form a line that holds, their archers loose volleys at the captain's horn, and the wastes charge in a mass; the engines' stones and pots strike where they land; each people's horns, war cries, the din of battle and battle music sound the war. Subtitles stay seven seconds or more and stack. Since W2 (D-069) the war changes the gate and the change is saved: the ram's crew swing it and it holes the barred leaf a stage at a time; the dwarves' mine is dug from behind the lines under the pass toward the tower on the barred side and the goblins' countermine from the yard to meet it, with sappers and goblins at the faces; ladders go up the towers and breaches are beaten through them; a siege tower is wheeled up with its drawbridge on the walk and soldiers climb to win the walk while the orcs pour pitch and drop stones; the orcs' pots set the works alight, which char and are rebuilt; graves and cairns fill the field behind the lines. When the four efforts are done the hosts' last assault takes the gate: the leaf shattered, the mine fired and the tower above it brought down, the orcs' camp burned, the peoples' banners on the other tower, victory fires in the Leaguer, and from then the hosts hold the yard and the walk. The war's fallen lie a few minutes, and crows come to them.

**The Ember Stair (D-067):** inside every great volcano a way winds up from a door in the Ember Throne's way in, round the mountain at one in six, two to three turns, keeping 18 blocks of rock over it, up to a gallery open over the crater's lake of fire. It alternates stretches of the orcs' stronghold (halls: the Gate of the Stair, barracks, a forge, an armoury, the Shrine of the Emberlord, a fighting pit) and raw cave of the mountain's fire (the Cavern of Cinders with its pool of lava, the Obsidian Hollow), each chamber held by orcs, goblins or trolls.

**The Volcanic Wastes remade (D-051):** a burnt land under a burning sky. The sky goes black with a red glow at the horizon, and the land lies in a dimmer, redder light that never turns its dark rock to black: the ash, cinder, basalt and obsidian are dark but show their grain and edges, and by night the burning smoke lights the land a dull red (D-059); the sun is a dull ember, the clouds are dark as smoke, and storms throw forked blue lightning, half of it into the plumes over the craters. Volcanoes (about 120 to 200 blocks across the foot and 30 to 70 high, where the wastes are wide enough to hold one) each have a name of their own ("Mount Quarvex"), a crater with a lake of lava held in rock, and two to four flows spilling through breaches in the rim and down the flanks in channels, with crusted older flows beside them. Rivers and lakes in the wastes hold lava and never meet water; nearer the sea they are obsidian. Fissures of lava split the plains. The ground is black ash, cinder rock and grey ash, with fields and seams of glowing lava crust, fumaroles ringed with sulphur that puff smoke, charred trees (some still smouldering), fallen charred logs, basalt spires and obsidian shards. Embers drift on the wind and rise from lava, lava bombs arc from the craters, and the land rumbles, crackles and booms. Signatures: the Ashen Citadel (a black fortress on a moat of lava, six towers with braziers and a spire crowned with fire forty blocks up) and the Rift of Fire (a chasm with a river of lava along its floor, spanned by a basalt arch).

**Lands and their layout (M6a, D-039):**
- **Cells:** the world is cut into cells about 320 blocks across, their centres shifted and their borders warped. Each cell takes one land from the climate at its centre: warmth (five bands), damp (three), relief (low, hills, high), and sea or coast. Warmth and damp change over thousands of blocks, so neighbouring cells share a climate.
- **Tiers (Q118):** common lands spread over several cells (median about 490 blocks on a side); uncommon lands are mostly one or two cells (about 340); rare lands are always one cell (about 330). No land is smaller than 215 x 215 blocks; all but the tips of corners lie within a 115-block circle of their own land.
- **The transition map (Q108):** two lands may border when their warmth bands are at most one apart and their damp at most one apart, unless one names the other as never; a cell never takes a land a settled neighbour may not border. No winter land meets a desert.
- **Stand-ins:** a land whose family is not built yet wears the look and name of an old land (Autumn Woods shows as Elder Wood, Glacier Fields as High Mountains, and so on), so each family's PR changes only how its lands look, never where they lie.
- **Names (Q131):** each stretch of a land (a cell and the neighbouring cells of the same land) has its own name in its people's style, such as "Elder Wood of Lirwen" or "The Sea of Randbert", shown on arrival and on the world map. The Grey Shore and lakes keep their plain names.
- **Heights blend** through the weights of nearby cells by look (`w2` to `w11`, and `wS` for the sea) over about 35 blocks either side of a border (55 at the sea and the moors, 110 at mountains); the patchy threshold still mixes trees, ground and snow across the band.

**A natural surface (M3.5b, D-031):**
- **Rivers follow valleys.** They rise in the hills and run down to the sea or a lake, never across a mountain range. The higher the land beside a river, the wider and gentler its valley; the channel shelves up into sandy or gravel banks. Before 0.10.0 a third of river stretches ran in ravines.
- **Mountain ranges** have long ridgelines with valleys between them and saddles where a range can be crossed. The high peaks stand on the ridges at the hearts of the ranges, and cliffs are rare.
- **Smooth ground with character:** gentle lands are about half as bumpy as before. The moors keep their rocky tors, the Shadowed Forest its hummocks, and the fens their pools.
- **Lands blend:** heights blend through the weights, and which land a column belongs to is decided against a patchy threshold, so trees, ground cover and snow mix over a wide border.
- **Placement:**
  - Boulders and tors lie on the moors, in the mountains and on steep slopes, and are rare on gentle ground.
  - Trees gather in groves with clearings between them, thicker in valleys and by water and thinner on steep slopes.
  - The stray little towers and wells are gone; the ruins on the surface are the named sites.


## The world's height (D-023)

The world is 512 blocks tall and sea level is y310, so the underground keeps its old depth and there are about 200 blocks of sky above the sea. The hearts of the High Mountains rise far above the old limit; snow starts at the snow line (y343 and up).

## The underground (D-028)

The underground is made of **cave systems**, planned rather than noise: in each area of 160 x 160 blocks, one to three systems. Each has a main passage (the trunk) that starts at an entrance on the surface and winds down through the depths, with side branches that end in a chamber, some loops back into the trunk, and drops down shafts. Passages only meet at junctions and keep at least three blocks of rock from everything else. There is far less hollow rock than before 0.9.0 (about 1.2 to 1.7% of the rock under spawn, against about 28%).

Size and character change gradually with depth (no hard layers):

| Depth | y | What you find |
|---|---|---|
| Near the surface | ~240 up | Passages mostly 3 to 6 wide (sometimes 2 to 4), small domed rooms, stream pools; cave regions (mossy, crystal, dripstone, fungal) |
| The middle depths | ~150 to 240 | Wider passages, halls of 16 to 34 blocks, rifts; rare old mineshafts, camps, alchemists' cellars, old smithies, dungeon rooms |
| The great caverns | ~60 to 150 | Great domed halls with natural pillars, tall rifts with ledges and high windows, stepped halls going down to lakes; old stone ruins and shrines; deepstone begins about y100 |
| The deep | ~20 to 60 | The widest passages and largest halls, other peoples' remains, gorges with lava at the bottom, lava falls. Under and around a hold: its city (floors 64 and 82) and mines (galleries at 20, 32 and 44, inclines, great stepped pits) |
| The Fire Below | 3 to ~24 | An open lava sea (lava y3 to y8) under most of the land, with smooth islands and great pillars flaring at both ends; low under the holds' mines. The main system of most areas reaches it by a causeway of fallen rock to an island |

Under the Volcanic Wastes the two upper bands are a goblin warren instead (D-052): under the sunken floor (D-055) its halls lie at about y 130 to 192, its tunnels reach up to the floor, and a way down leads to the caves below, which begin under it.

Ways between depths: ramps, steeper passages and spirals (all walkable), shafts and chasms to climb or drop, streams of pools stepping down a passage, and old built ways (stairs, mine inclines). The deepest halls of neighbouring areas are often linked, and near a hold a passage leads into the upper gallery of its mines.

**Underground water and lava are always held in sound rock** (owner's rule, D-024, Q87): water or solid under and beside every block, the floor under it resting on more rock, the walls beside it resting on something. Lakes lie in stepped halls, pools along streams. Lava lies only in the sea, in gorges that open onto it, in small pools under lava falls, and in the holds' forges; a lava fall pours from a slot in the rock into its pool, the only open face it has.

## Ways down (D-024, D-028)

- **Cave entrances**: a mouth at the foot of a slope, or a sinkhole on level ground with a ramp spiralling down its wall, into the trunk of a cave system.
- **Ruined stairways** stand on level ground at the foot of a slope: a broken stone shaft with a stair winding down to a cave passage (rare since 0.9.0).
- **Gorges** (since 0.15.0, Q137; once the hairline ravines) cut along a slow line across hilly and high lands, never by water or the sea: a floor at least 7 blocks wide, walls stepping back in ledges of 5 to a rim 16 to 40 across, up to about 60 blocks deep (the deepest reach the crawlways), with square ends that ease out. About 0.6% of land; a third as many as the old ravines.
- **Hold gates** lead down into the holds (below).
A way down is usually within 50 to 70 blocks of open land.

## Places underground (D-024)

- **Dungeon rooms** (about 1 chunk in 80): Forgotten Crypt, Old Storeroom, Old Cells, Sunken Chapel, each opening onto a cave passage.
- **Points of interest** (about 1 chunk in 18): miners' camps, alchemists' cellars, deep outposts, lava forges, crystal shrines, ancient ruins and mushroom groves open onto a cave passage; crystal geodes and fossils are sealed in the rock.
- **Remains of other peoples** stand in great halls away from the holds (about one area of 160 x 160 blocks in three): goblin warrens, gnome workshops, drow halls, and nameless ruins older than the holds. About a third are only leftovers.

## The surface (D-024)

- **Ruined sites** of the men of old, on level ground in a commanding spot (about two areas of 384 x 384 blocks in three; towers and castles on high ground, keeps by a river; never in a hollow), on an earth bank sloping down to the land around: round watchtowers, walled keeps with corner towers, castles with a curtain wall and a keep. Each has a name.
- **Old roads** of worn path, stones and gravel wander between neighbouring sites and up to the hold gates.
- **Ancient waystones** stand at about a third of sites and at the first gate of every hold. Attuning to them comes in M4.

## Old lights

The old peoples' lamps went out long ago. Lanterns, sconces, torches and glowstone in ruins, mines, camps and towers are found cold (Cold Lantern, Cold Sconce, Burnt-out Torch, Dim Glowstone); coal relights the first three. Rune stones, crystals and glowing fungi and moss still shine. Inhabited holds keep their lamps burning.

## Dwarven holds

- **Rare and vast** (Q9): one hold in each region of 2560 x 2560 blocks, about 400 to 575 blocks across and about 2000 blocks from the next. None lies under the spawn area; the nearest is usually 1500 to 2500 blocks away. Each hold has one name.
- **Some are inhabited** (about 3 in 10): kept up and lit, but empty until settlements and their peoples arrive (E2). The rest are ruins.
- A hold is made of quarters of 8 x 8 chunks, organised into districts (residential, industrial, sacred, royal) with themed rooms and loot, on two floors (y64 and y82).
- Avenues wind from each quarter's edge to a central plaza; plazas carry waymarkers.
- Great structures span 2 x 2 chunks; the Hall of a Thousand Pillars spans 3 x 3 and both floors, a few per hold.
- Decay is heavy in abandoned holds (ruin intensity 0.55 to 1) and light in inhabited ones. Despite that, every room must be reachable: doorways open on both sides, every room has a doorway route toward an avenue, rubble is cleared between each doorway and the room's middle, and a tidy pass removes anything floating or leaking.
- **Mines are tied to holds** (Q12): galleries and pits run under the whole hold and thin out to nothing by about twice its radius. Stairwells on lower-floor avenues lead down into them.
- **Hold gates** (up to three per hold) stand on the highest ground over the hold's outer ring: a terrace with pillars and braziers over a spiral stair that climbs from the upper floor (y82). Waymarkers in the plazas point to the nearest gate. The first gate has an Ancient Waystone.

## Look of the world (D-027)

Sky light reaches sideways under overhangs and into cave mouths, fading over about 15 blocks. Water shimmers and catches the sun, the lava sea rolls, plants sway, and lamps and glowing blocks show through fog. Clouds are flat blocky shapes that stay in place in the world and drift slowly, coloured by sunset and dark at night. The moon goes through eight phases, one per day.

## Tools and gear (D-032)

- **The metal ladder:** wood, stone, copper, bronze, iron, steel, moonsilver. Each metal makes a pickaxe, an axe and a shovel, each faster and longer lasting than the metal before. Each pickaxe is the first that can mine the next metal's ore (stone for copper and zinc, copper for tin and gold, bronze for iron, iron for platinum and diamonds, steel for moonsilver, moonsilver for obsidian). Bronze is copper and tin; brass is copper and zinc; steel is iron and coal; moonsilver needs the blast furnace.
- **Gold and platinum** are for special things: blocks to build with, the gold sickle (cuts all plants within two blocks, harvests and replants ripe crops), the platinum pickaxe (fastest made, soon worn). Later currency and magic (Q68).
- **Bronze shears** keep leaves, cobwebs and plants whole.
- **Climbing:** ladders lean on a wall and stand on the ground or a ladder; iron pitons go into rock at any height; rope unrolls down from where it is placed; the grapnel, thrown at a wall below a ledge, catches the top edge and hangs rope to the ground. On any of them jump climbs, down or sprint climbs down, and letting go holds still.
- **Finding the way:** in survival a map shows the minimap, a compass gives X, Z and the heading, a depth gauge the height. Creative shows all of it.
- **Signal flares** climb about 60 blocks and burn red as they drift down.
- **The Blasting Keg** is the dwarves' mining charge. **The Blueprint Tool** is for creative mode only.
- **Swords and armour (D-052):** a sword on every step of the ladder (two of the material and a stick), the best weapon against the foes; axes come next. Armour goes in the new body slot: a Hide Jerkin (leather), Iron Mail, Steel Plate and Moonsilver Mail take 15, 30, 45 and 55 in a hundred of every blow from a foe and wear as they do. A troll's hide cures into three leather. The Emberlord's Flamebrand is the strongest blade.
- **Starting kit** for a new survival world: wooden pickaxe and axe, a map, 8 torches, 4 bread.

## Survival systems (D-033)

- **Containers:** Oak Chests you build, and the supply crates, dwarven chests and barrels of the old world, keep items by place. World containers roll their loot the first time they are opened and keep what is left. A container must be empty to be broken.
- **Room:** 36 slots; a Woven Pack (+9) or Sturdy Pack (+18) in the pack slot and a Satchel (+9) in the bag slot.
- **Light:** in survival the only light around you is a Miner's Lantern in the belt slot (burns lamp oil or pitch candles, only in the dark) or a light in your hand. The caves grow darker with depth. Darkness is atmosphere, never damage (Q13).
- **Travel:** waystone to waystone in survival. Attune Ancient Waystones by touch; build Carved Waystones at a high cost. Creative travels freely.
- **Lava flows like water** (D-053): opened, it spreads up to four blocks across a floor and falls without limit, slower than water, and makes no new sources. Where lava and water meet, the lava hardens: a source into obsidian, flowing lava into basalt.
- **Food:** wheat, potatoes, turnips and beans to farm; bilberries, brown mushrooms, wild turnips and apples to forage; bread, baked potatoes, roast turnips, pottage, bilberry tart and roast mushrooms to cook.

## Controls

Keyboard and mouse, touch, and Xbox-layout controllers (including the ROG Ally X in gamepad mode). The help panel on the pause screen switches to whichever is in use. Creative mode has flying and noclip (fly through blocks: N, the Clip button, or the pause menu). The pause menu's Resolution choice (Auto, Sharp, Fast) sets how sharply the world is drawn (D-034). The world map (M) shows everywhere you have explored with places, waystones and your markers; underground the minimap shows the layer at your feet. Creative adds block search, brushes to 13 wide, the Fill Tool, and pause-menu time, weather, coordinates and test structures (D-037). Every control can be rebound in the pause menu's Controls panel (the controller's A, B, triggers and left stick click are fixed); the help shows the controls in use for the device and mode, and one-time hints explain survival as it happens (D-036).

**How far you see (D-057):** the world is kept in memory as a square about you, 224, 320 or 416 blocks a side (the Loaded area setting: Near, Far, Farthest; Auto takes Far on desktops and handhelds with 8 cores or more), and the view reaches nearly to its edge: up to 104, 152 or 200 blocks, by the View setting (Auto, Near, Normal, Far, Farther, Farthest) or the frame rate. Beyond it, only the wastes' stand-in, the plumes and the sky.
